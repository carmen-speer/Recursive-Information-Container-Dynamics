What the score means, and what it doesn't. This number is the classifier's posterior estimate that an institution's current observed dynamics sit inside the modeled collapse regime -- not a calibrated probability that the institution will actually close. Below is what's currently known about each score below, institution by institution, not just a general caveat.

Likely classifier artifacts (fix written, not yet re-run against live data): University of Houston (87.3%), University of Central Florida (95.4%), and Florida State University (87.1%) are the clearest cases. frac_high_entropy measures the size of a resource swing, not its direction, so a large positive shock and a large destabilizing one look alike to it. Houston received a real $1.3B infusion from the Texas University Fund in this same period -- S&P upgraded its bond rating to AA+, citing a $287M operating surplus and $3.3B in reserves -- the opposite of distress. UCF and FSU both carry real, confirmed stable bond ratings, neither on negative outlook (Florida Board of Governors filing, 2/27/26), directly contradicting these high-risk scores. A fix -- gating frac_high_entropy on the already-signed debt_spike feature -- was written and re-validated against the 54-institution panel on 2026-09-19, but has not yet been re-run against these three institutions' live data; that requires a fresh run of the automated re-scoring pipeline.

Same suspected mechanism, not yet checked against real-world evidence: University at Buffalo (80.7%) shows the same high-frac_high_entropy pattern as the three above, but no bond rating or other independent financial evidence has been checked against it yet -- its status is genuinely unresolved, not confirmed either way.

Likely genuine signals: Clemson University (72.3%) and California State University-Long Beach (98.6%) have real, independently documented problems behind their scores -- Clemson's long-term liabilities are up $231.9M year-over-year to $2.65B with expenses outpacing revenue; Long Beach has a real, documented enrollment collapse (38,000 to 36,000 students, projected toward 33,000) alongside a real $42M budget cut. Nothing here contradicts either score.

A different, still-unbuilt gap: University of Phoenix-Arizona (100.0%) and Sweet Briar College (52.4%) sit on a separate problem from the one above: the model has no state for "collapse, but already reset." Phoenix is mid-restructuring after a real, large enrollment contraction; Sweet Briar underwent a real near-closure and recovery in 2015. Both scores may be reading the aftermath of a completed disruption as ongoing collapse -- unlike the frac_high_entropy fix above, this fix has not yet been designed or built.

Unexamined against either gap: West Virginia University (68.0%) underwent a real, widely-reported financial crisis and program/faculty cuts starting in 2023 -- plausibly a genuine signal -- but has not been specifically checked against either gap described above.

None of this should be read as "this institution is X% likely to close." Full detail is in the repository's Known Gaps section.
